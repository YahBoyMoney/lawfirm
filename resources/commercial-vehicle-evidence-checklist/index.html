A crash involving a truck, a delivery van, a company vehicle, or a rideshare driver is rarely a two-party event. Behind the driver there can be a carrier, a broker, a vehicle owner, a maintenance vendor, a staffing company, and more than one insurer.

Much of the proof is electronic, held by those companies, and subject to retention schedules that keep it for a limited time. What you capture in the first days can affect what is still available later.

Crash scene and vehicle proof

Preserve what identifies the event and every organization connected to it.

  • Photographs of the scene, both vehicles, cargo, road conditions, skid marks, and debris
  • License plates, USDOT and MC numbers, company markings, trailer numbers, and placards
  • Driver name, license, employer, and the app or trip details if a platform was involved
  • The report number, the responding agency, and the names of any responding units
  • Witness names and phone numbers, plus nearby businesses that may have cameras

Carrier, platform, and vehicle records

These are the records you cannot recreate later.

  • Dispatch, trip, and load documents connected to the run
  • Hiring, training, qualification, and supervision records for the driver
  • Maintenance, inspection, repair, brake, and tire service history for the vehicle
  • Leasing, ownership, permission, and registration documents
  • Insurance information for the driver, the vehicle owner, and the company

Electronic data with short retention windows

Ask early. Many of these systems overwrite on a schedule.

  • Electronic logging device and hours-of-service data
  • Telematics, GPS, and fleet-management records
  • Event data recorder information from the vehicles involved
  • Dash camera footage, in-cab video, and rideshare app trip data
  • Traffic, transit, business, and residential camera video near the location

Treatment, work impact, and communications

Build a date-indexed record without altering originals.

  • Every treatment date, provider, restriction, and follow-up instruction
  • Bills, explanations of benefits, and out-of-pocket costs
  • Missed work, reduced hours, and any written employer communication
  • All insurance letters, requests for statements, and requests to sign anything
  • Known government, claim, insurer, or court dates

Preserve these before anything else

  • The vehicle itself, before it is repaired, sold, or scrapped
  • Damaged safety equipment, child seats, helmets, devices, and clothing
  • Original photo and video files with their date information intact
  • Every insurer letter, estimate, and request for a recorded statement

Keep originals intact, work from copies, and do not send sensitive records through a public form.

What to expect

What the first review actually does.

  1. An early preservation discussion

    Which records may be time-sensitive, and which organizations may hold them, is one of the first things reviewed.

  2. A party map

    Driver, employer, owner, broker, platform, maintenance vendor, and insurers are identified as separate potential sources of responsibility and coverage.

  3. A proof and damages review

    Scene proof, medical documentation, work impact, and vehicle evidence are reviewed together rather than one at a time.

  4. A next-step decision

    The firm may accept, refer, co-counsel, or decline after conflicts and fit review. Contact alone is not representation.

Frequently asked

Common questions about this guide.

How quickly can commercial-vehicle records disappear?

Retention periods vary by company and by system, and some electronic records are kept for a short window before being overwritten. That is why preservation is raised at the very beginning rather than after a claim is filed.

The other driver said the company would handle it. Is that enough?

A verbal assurance is not a record. Get the claim number, the company name, the adjuster contact, and any promise in writing, and keep documenting your own treatment and losses in the meantime.

Does a rideshare crash work the same way?

The evidence map is similar, but trip status, app data, and the platform's own insurance structure add records that are held by the platform. Save the trip receipt, screenshots, and driver information immediately.
