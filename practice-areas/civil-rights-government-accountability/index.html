What is at stake

Why this cannot sit on a shelf.

A public agency or a government actor caused harm, and the records that would prove it are held by the same institution you are questioning.

  • Claims involving public entities can carry separate notice steps that run early.
  • Body camera, dispatch, jail, and facility video is often kept only for a set retention period.
  • Records requests take time, and the request history itself becomes part of the record.

What to expect

What the first review actually does.

  1. Conflicts and parties first

    Everyone on every side is identified before a closer review, because a conflict controls whether the firm can look at the matter at all.

  2. A timing screen, not a deadline calculation

    Known dates, notices, and procedural steps are reviewed against your facts. No web page and no first call can promise you a filing date.

  3. A preservation list

    What may disappear first is identified early, including records held by an employer, an insurer, an agency, or a company.

Frequently asked

Civil Rights and Government Accountability questions.

Is a claim against a city or county different from a normal lawsuit?

Matters involving public entities can involve additional procedural steps and separate timing that do not apply to private defendants. The specific steps depend on the entity and the claim, so the sequence should be reviewed early rather than assumed.

How do I keep agency video from being erased?

Agencies keep recordings for limited retention periods that vary by department and system. Write down the date, time, location, and any incident or report number, and raise preservation as early as possible so a request can be evaluated.

Can I bring a claim if no one was arrested or charged?

Charging decisions are made by prosecutors and are a separate question from a civil claim. What matters for a civil review is the conduct, the harm, the available proof, and the applicable procedure.
